# Reference — *Zouden* (the Dutch "would")

A complete overview of **zouden**, the conditional auxiliary. This sheet is written in **English** for quick reference; it consolidates the material spread across **S16** (*ik stuur je wel een sms-je*) and **S17a** (*zouden in depth*).

---

## *Zullen* vs. *zouden* — don't mix them up

| Auxiliary | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **zullen** | future / offer | *Ik **zal** je helpen.* (I will help you.) · ***Zullen** we gaan?* (Shall we go?) |
| **zouden** | conditional / polite / hypothetical | everything below |

---

## The five uses of *zouden*

### 1. Conditional / hypothetical ("if…then")
The English **"would"** in an unreal or imagined situation.

- *Als ik meer geld **had**, **zou** ik een reis maken.* — If I had more money, I would take a trip.
- *Als het mooi weer **was**, **zouden** we buiten zitten.* — If the weather were nice, we'd sit outside.
- *Als ik de loterij **zou** winnen, **zou** ik stoppen met werken.* — If I were to win the lottery, I'd stop working.

> Both clauses can carry *zou* (*als … zou … zou*) when the condition is itself hypothetical.

### 2. Polite requests
Softens a request — the equivalent of "would you…?" / "could you…?"

- *Zou u dat kunnen herhalen?* — Could you repeat that?
- *Zou u mij kunnen helpen?* — Could you help me?

### 3. Reminder of a plan ("was/were going to…")
Refers back to something that had been arranged.

- *Je **zou** nog bellen.* — You were going to call.
- *We **zouden** om zeven uur afspreken.* — We were going to meet at seven.

### 4. Giving advice
- *Je **zou** wat vroeger naar bed moeten gaan.* — You should go to bed a bit earlier.

### 5. Expressing a wish / soft preference
- *Ik **zou** graag een afspraak willen maken.* — I would like to make an appointment.

---

## The polite combo: *zou + willen / kunnen*

Adding *willen* or *kunnen* makes the request extra polite — no *alstublieft* needed:

- *Zou je dit voor me **willen** faxen?* — Would you mind faxing this?
- *Zou ik je pen **kunnen** lenen?* — Could I borrow your pen?
- *Zou je meneer Jansen voor me willen opbellen?* — Could you phone Mr. Jansen for me?

---

## Word order in conditional sentences (S17a)

**Structure:** **Als** + [subject + … + verb **at the end**], [main clause with **zou(den)** in position 2].

- *Als ik geen auto **had**, **zou** ik de trein **nemen**.*

Two rules to remember:
- In the *als*-clause, the verb always goes **to the end**.
- In the main clause, *zou(den)* sits in **position 2**.

## Conjugation

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| ik **zou** | wij / we **zouden** |
| jij / je **zou** | jullie **zouden** |
| u **zou** | zij / ze **zouden** |
| hij / zij / het **zou** | |

## Quick self-check

| You want to… | Use |
|--------------|-----|
| describe an unreal "if…then" | *als … zou(den) …* |
| ask politely | *Zou u …?* / *Zou je … willen/kunnen?* |
| remind someone of a plan | *Je zou nog …* |
| give advice | *Je zou … moeten …* |
| state the plain future or an offer | **zullen**, not zouden |
